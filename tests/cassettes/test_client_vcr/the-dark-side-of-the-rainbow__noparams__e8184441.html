Synopsis:
A simple mashup of Pink Floyd's "The Dark Side of the Moon" with the classic 1939 film "The Wizard of Oz", including both stereo and surround sound options for the album along with the original movie audio. Three subtitle tracks are included: a description of each time the movie and music sync up, the lyrics to the album, and the original movie subtitles.
Intention:
About 20 years ago, I made a custom DVD for my friends to watch "The Wizard of Oz" synced up with Pink Floyd's album "The Dark Side of the Moon". It was one of my first edits and it's pretty messy; I messed up the frame rate conversion from the DVD, and the audio was only in stereo. It's time for this edit to get an upgrade, with Blu-ray video and the 5.1 surround mix of the album.
Additional Notes:
This idea first appeared online in the early 1990s on a Pink Floyd newsgroup, and was popularized by Charles Savage in a 1995 newspaper article written for the Fort Wayne Journal Gazette. The list of synchronizations provided in the subtitles were sourced from an internet bulletin board in the early 2000s, probably long gone at this point. Unfortunately, I didn't write down who documented them and am unable to provide credit here.
Other Sources:
The Wizard of Oz 75th Anniversary Blu-ray
The Dark Side of the Moon CD (stereo)
The Dark Side of the Moon SAC (surround)

Review
I will start by admitting that I have inside knowledge that this is a diamond in the rough: an inspired fan edit waiting to be completed to perfection, which I will address further when I explain my enjoyment rating. (If this admission is cause for offense, I hope you can forgive me, asterixsmeagol.)

I really appreciate this edit for being much more accessible than playing the album and the movie simultaneously. Removing the clash of the original music is an obvious improvement in the enjoyment factor.

The addition of the subtitle explanations at each moment of synchronicity is enlightening. One may be tempted to downplay some of the claimed points of sync, but considering the totality of syncs on the balance of probabilities, it becomes very difficult to believe the band's claim that they had no intention to sync their album to the movie. And even so, asterixsmeagol does not take credit for the claims of the subtitles, but has instead done the work to integrate a decades-old compilation of alleged syncs into the edit, and in the most easily accessible way for the viewer. Much better than following along with written notes!

I have deducted one star for narrative because I believe the edit would be even better with the original dialogue included in the subtitles. I have seen the movie many times, but I have not memorized the dialogue, which I suspect is true for almost every viewer.

Now my confession: I have deducted one star for enjoyment due to - of all things - the kindness of asterixsmeagol. In direct message, this editor disclosed to me the aspiration for a future update to this edit. I performed a rough simulation of that plan in one scene and IT WORKS BRILLIANTLY. And so, due to the editor's kindness, I cannot help but hold the knowledge that a perfect 10 version is waiting to be released. And I am repaying that kindness with a small snub. I was torn about doing this, but I hope I will be able to return at a later date and update this review to a perfect 10 after asterixsmeagol brings the vision into reality!
